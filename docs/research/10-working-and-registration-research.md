# 10 — Working in the UK during and after a medical degree (2027 entry cohort)

Research session: 2026-10-03. Audience: content team for StudyMedicineUKNigeria.com.
Status of every fact below: **VERIFY-ON-PAGE** (located through web-search snippets; official pages were not fetched directly). See `docs/DATA-AVAILABILITY.md`.

**Standing caveat for all site copy derived from this file:** studying medicine in the UK does not give a right to practise, a right to a visa, or a guarantee of a training post or job. Registration decisions belong to the GMC; visa decisions belong to UKVI; Foundation and specialty allocations are competitive and subject to rules that are changing.

## 2. Findings — Student visa work rules (during the degree)

- FACT (GOV.UK, via snippets and university immigration pages): Student visa holders on a degree-level course at a higher-education provider may work up to **20 hours per week during term time** and **full time during official vacations** (no upper hourly limit outside term). Paid work and volunteering hours count together toward the 20-hour cap.
  - SOURCES: https://www.gov.uk/student-visa (official; the "your visa conditions" sub-page not captured) ; https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/blog/7093/Navigating-work-and-study-with-a-Student-visa (UKCISA) ; university explainers: https://www.exeter.ac.uk/international-students/visas/student/working-during-study/ ; https://www.westminster.ac.uk/international/visas-and-advice/visas/student-visa/working-in-the-uk-with-a-student-visa ; https://gold.ac.uk/immigration/during/working-while-studying
- FACT (same sources): prohibited — self-employment/freelance work, professional sportsperson or coach, entertainer, a permanent full-time vacancy. Conditions appear on the visa/eVisa.
- INFERENCE for medicine: medical courses have long terms and placements; many medical schools advise students to limit paid work. The site should present the 20-hour rule as a legal maximum, not a planning assumption, and warn that term/vacation definitions are set by the university (medical-school term dates often differ from the rest of the university).
- Nurses/healthcare-assistant bank work during vacation is common for medical students and is permitted as employment (not self-employment) — INFERENCE; confirm with the university's immigration team.

## 3. Findings — Graduate visa (post-study work)

- FACT (reported from the Statement of Changes of 14 Oct 2025): for Graduate visa applications made **on or after 1 January 2027**, the grant is **18 months** (previously 2 years). Applications made on or before 31 Dec 2026 still receive 2 years. Doctoral graduates keep 3 years.
  - LEAD sources (consistent with each other): https://monitor.icef.com/2025/10/uk-to-implement-reduced-graduate-route-from-january-2027/ ; https://www.davidsonmorris.com/graduate-route-reduced-to-18-months/ ; https://www.tlt.com/insights-and-events/insight/next-tranche-of-immigration-changes-published ; https://www.hextonslaw.com/post/graduate-visa-cut-to-18-months-from-january-2027-what-current-students-need-to-know
  - GOV.UK Statement of Changes page itself: **NOT FOUND IN THIS SESSION** — must be cited directly before publishing.
- INFERENCE — does it apply to someone starting medicine in September 2027? Yes in practice: the rule is keyed to the **date of the Graduate visa application**, not the course start date. A 2027 entrant graduates in 2032 (5-year course) or 2033, so any Graduate visa they apply for would fall under the 18-month rule (or whatever rule is then in force).
- INFERENCE — relevance to medical graduates specifically: most UK medical graduates who need sponsorship go straight from Student visa to a sponsored work visa for Foundation training (section 4.3). The Graduate visa is a fallback (e.g. if no Foundation post is secured, or to bridge a gap). It allows work at any skill level but does not count toward settlement.
- Fees:
  - Application fee: University of Edinburgh immigration page states **£880** for applications on/after 9 April 2025 (https://registryservices.ed.ac.uk/immigration/working-in-the-uk/after-studies/graduate-visa/costs-graduate-visa — AUTHORITATIVE-adjacent). One LEAD source claims **£937** from 8 April 2026 (https://visaeta.uk/blog/graduate-visa-2026-guide/). **Conflict — NOT RESOLVED IN THIS SESSION; confirm on GOV.UK.**
  - Immigration Health Surcharge: **£1,035 per year** of visa (LEAD + Edinburgh page). Edinburgh reports a 2-year Graduate visa totalling £2,950 at the 2025 rates (£880 + 2 × £1,035).
- English requirement for Graduate route: LEAD reports a move to B2 from January 2026 (section 2.1) — VERIFY. For UK medical graduates this is unlikely to bind (degree taught in English), INFERENCE.

## 4. Findings — GMC registration and Foundation training for UK medical graduates

### 4.1 Provisional registration and the MLA

- FACT (university regulations/course pages quoting GMC policy): from the 2024–25 academic year all UK medical students must pass the **Medical Licensing Assessment (MLA)** — the Applied Knowledge Test (AKT) and the Clinical and Professional Skills Assessment (CPSA) — as part of their degree before they are eligible to join the medical register.
  - SOURCES: https://www.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/policy/regulations-current/regulations2025-26/mvls/mbchb/ ; https://www.st-andrews.ac.uk/subjects/medicine/medicine-bsc-a100/after-graduation/ ; https://medicine-vet-medicine.ed.ac.uk/edinburgh-medical-school/medicine/the-student-experience/outcome
  - gmc-uk.org MLA and registration pages: **NOT FOUND IN THIS SESSION** (budget exhausted) — the GMC page must be the cited source on the site.
- FACT (same sources): graduates of a UK primary medical qualification are entitled to apply for **provisional registration with a licence to practise**, subject to a fitness-to-practise declaration (not more than three months old). Provisional registration is time-limited (reported maximum of three years and 30 days / 1,125 days).
- FACT (INFERENCE from the above): nationality is not a criterion for provisional registration; a Nigerian graduate of a UK medical school follows the same GMC route as a UK national. Full registration follows satisfactory completion of Foundation Year 1 (F1). GMC fees: NOT FOUND IN THIS SESSION.

### 4.2 UK Foundation Programme (UKFP) eligibility

- FACT (UKFPO): UKFP 2027 eligibility and right-to-work guidance is published at
  - https://foundationprogramme.nhs.uk/programmes/2-year-foundation-programme/eligibility-information/ukfp-2027-eligibility-applications-what-you-will-need/right-to-work/
  - https://foundationprogramme.nhs.uk/programmes/2-year-foundation-programme/ukfp/how-to-apply-on-oriel/eligibility/
- FACT (UKFPO, via snippet): applicants who are not UK/settled workers must provide evidence of right to work for the duration of the two-year programme **after allocation**, not at the point of application; after allocation, Certificates of Sponsorship are arranged for applicants who need a work visa. Applicants indicate on Oriel: "I wish to be sponsored".
- FACT (UKFPO, via snippet): the page states that at time of publication all medical specialties are on the Shortage Occupation List, so the Resident Labour Market Test does not apply. (Note: the SOL has since been replaced by the Immigration Salary List and the RLMT abolished in 2020 — the UKFPO wording may be legacy; VERIFY current wording.)
- INFERENCE: final-year students at UK medical schools apply to UKFP in the autumn of final year regardless of nationality; the UK-graduate application route (via the medical school) is open to international students at UK schools. The exact 2027 timeline (application window, allocation date) was NOT FOUND IN THIS SESSION.

### 4.3 Visa sponsorship for F1

- FACT (BMA + NHS regional guidance, via snippets): international graduates of UK medical schools who need sponsorship for Foundation training are sponsored on the **Health and Care Worker visa** (a Skilled Worker sub-route). After allocation the relevant sponsorship team contacts those who need sponsorship with access to an overseas sponsorship portal to obtain the Certificate of Sponsorship.
  - SOURCES: https://www.bma.org.uk/advice-and-support/international-doctors/training-in-the-uk/visa-guide-for-international-doctors-and-students-training-in-the-uk ; https://www.bma.org.uk/advice-and-support/international-doctors-and-students/studying-and-training-in-the-uk/training-in-the-uk-and-your-visa ; NHS Wessex process note: https://wessex.hee.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2025/03/Visa-application-process-for-Foundation-doctors.pdf
- Health and Care Worker visa fee (LEAD, 2026): £324 (up to 3 years) / £628 (over 3 years), reported from 8 April 2026; IHS exemption applies to this route; English B2 for new applicants from 8 January 2026 (LEAD: https://uksponsors.com/blog/health-care-worker-visa-guide). **VERIFY on GOV.UK.** NHS Employers guidance page NOT FOUND IN THIS SESSION.
- INFERENCE: the Student → Health and Care Worker switch is made in the UK after allocation and before F1 start (early August). The Graduate visa is the fallback if no post is allocated.

### 4.4 Prioritisation of UK graduates — official vs proposed (carefully labelled)

What is **official (published by Government/NHS England/Parliament)**:
- FACT: the **10 Year Health Plan for England** (July 2025) committed to prioritising UK medical graduates for Foundation training and to prioritising UK graduates and doctors with significant NHS experience for specialty training, and to creating 1,000 new specialty posts over three years.
  - SOURCES: House of Lords Library: https://lordslibrary.parliament.uk/competition-for-specialist-training-programmes-in-the-nhs-sector-views-and-the-governments-plans-for-resident-doctor-training-posts/ ; Pulse report: https://www.pulsetoday.co.uk/news/pulse-careers/nhs-10-year-plan-will-prioritise-uk-medical-graduates-for-speciality-training/
- FACT: a **Medical Training (Prioritisation) Bill** (2024–26 session) has been introduced to implement that commitment. NHS England has published information for applicants about it.
  - SOURCES: NHS England: https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/medical-training-prioritisation-bill-information-for-applicants-to-medical-training/ ; Commons Library briefing CBP-10473: https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-10473/CBP-10473.pdf ; Explanatory notes: https://bills.parliament.uk/publications/64595/documents/7710 ; Impact statement: https://bills.parliament.uk/publications/64705/documents/7764 ; BMA position: https://www.bma.org.uk/news-and-opinion/priority-access-to-specialty-training-for-uk-medical-graduates
- FACT (reported from the Bill's explanatory material, via snippet): for specialty posts starting **from 2026**, prioritisation at **offer** stage of UK medical graduates, a defined "priority group", and those who completed the UK Foundation Programme or equivalent; from **2027**, broader prioritisation at **interview and offer** stage covering graduates of UK and Republic of Ireland medical schools, those who completed UK foundation training, and those with certain immigration statuses.
- **Royal Assent / commencement status as of October 2026: NOT FOUND IN THIS SESSION.** Do not describe the Bill as law until checked at bills.parliament.uk.

What is **not established in this session**:
- Whether "graduate of a UK medical school" in the Bill/implementing rules includes **international students** who graduated from UK schools (the explanatory-note wording above suggests the category is defined by place of qualification, not nationality, but the exact definition and any immigration-status criterion must be read on the Bill text). **NOT CONFIRMED.**
- Whether Foundation (UKFP) allocation rules for 2027/2028 have changed as a result — NOT FOUND.

INFERENCE for a Nigerian student entering a UK medical school in 2027:
- Under the proposals as reported, a UK-qualified graduate is in a *more* favourable position than an overseas-qualified IMG for Foundation and specialty recruitment. Whether international UK graduates sit in the top priority tier or a lower tier depends on definitions that must be read from the Bill/regulations. The site must not promise priority.
- The direction of policy (White Paper 2025, 10 Year Plan, this Bill) is toward favouring UK-trained doctors and shortening post-study routes; a 2027 entrant will graduate into rules written in 2026–2032. Date-stamp everything.

## 6. What this means for a Nigerian applicant (INFERENCE)

1. During the degree: up to 20 hours/week paid work in term, full-time in vacations, no self-employment, no dependants on an undergraduate Student visa. Maintenance evidence for the visa is rising (reported £1,203/month outside London from 30 Nov 2026 — verify); budget for the full 9 months plus first-year fees.
2. After graduating (2032–33): the normal path is MLA passed within the degree → GMC provisional registration → UKFP F1 with Health and Care Worker visa sponsorship arranged after allocation. Nationality does not bar any of these steps; none is guaranteed.
3. The Graduate visa (18 months for applications from 1 Jan 2027) is a safety net, not the main route for doctors.
4. UK-graduate prioritisation is official government policy and a Bill is in progress; whether international UK graduates share the top tier is unresolved in this session. Present this as "policy in flux", with the NHS England applicant page linked.
5. Every figure in this file will change at least once before a 2027 entrant graduates. The site should teach students *where* to check (GOV.UK, GMC, UKFPO, NHS England) rather than memorise numbers.
