## Business position this document is written for

- The business has an institutional *contact* at one UK university. It holds **no signed agent agreement, representative agreement, referral agreement, or letter of appointment** with any institution.
- Until such a document exists, the site must not describe itself as an "agent", "partner", "representative", "authorised/official representative", "affiliate" or "approved" by any university, by UCAS, by the British Council, or by any regulator.

## 1. Definitions and differences

### 1.1 Education agent (recruitment agent)

- `FACT` The UK *National Code of Ethical Practice for UK Education Agents* "sets out the standards and best practice that education agents and agent aggregators, brokers and marketplaces representing UK education providers are expected to adhere to." It describes itself as having been "born out of the London Statement (2012) and the British Council Good Practice Guide for Education Agents (2013)." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.buila.ac.uk/uploads/docs/National-Code-of-Ethical-Practice-for-UK-Education-Agents-FINAL-Flag-version-2.pdf ; mirror: https://universitiesuk.ac.uk/sites/default/files/uploads/UUKi/Documents%20-%20misc/National%20Code%20of%20Ethical%20Practice%20for%20UK%20Education%20Agents.pdf
- `FACT` The British Council describes the National Code as applying to "education agents and agent aggregators, brokers and marketplaces (digital agent networks) representing UK education providers **on the register of licensed student sponsors**." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.britishcouncil.org/education/agents-counsellors/uk-quality-agent-framework
- `INFERENCE` The defining feature of an *agent* in this ecosystem is that it **represents a UK provider** under a contract with that provider (normally commission-bearing). A business that advises students but has no contract with any provider is not, in this sense, an education agent — and must not describe itself as one.

### 1.2 Recruitment partner / recruitment agency (commission-based)

- `FACT` Universities publish "become an agent" processes. Birmingham City University's process asks applicants to complete an application form, give contact details for two references, provide proof of education-agent training or qualifications, proof of business registration in the agent's country, and proof of academic qualifications or professional recognition. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.bcu.ac.uk/international/policies-agents-partnerships/agent-portal/agents-process ; https://www.bcu.ac.uk/international/other-info/agents-process
- `FACT` Where an application is approved the university "send[s] the agent an agreement for signature" and sets student targets (search snippet summarising the BCU/Northumbria pages). `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://corp.northumbria.ac.uk/international/agent-information
- `INFERENCE` "Recruitment partner" and "recruitment agency" are marketing synonyms for a contracted, commission-paid education agent. The word "partner" implies a contractual relationship with the university and should be avoided until one exists.

### 1.3 Referral partner / introducer

- `DATA UNAVAILABLE` No authoritative UK education-sector definition of "referral partner" or "introducer" was retrieved before the search budget was exhausted. `NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION`
- `INFERENCE` In general commercial usage an *introducer* or *referral partner* is paid a fee for introducing a prospective customer but has **no authority to act on behalf of, bind, or speak for** the principal. Some universities run referral or "introducer" arrangements distinct from full agency agreements; whether the contact university does so is unknown. Even a referral arrangement is a **contract** and would need to be documented before any "referral partner" wording is used.
- `RECOMMENDATION` Do not use "referral partner" or "introducer" on the site until a signed referral agreement exists. If the business merely signposts a university without any agreement, say "we can point you to [University] admissions" — not "we refer students to [University]".

### 1.4 Representative / authorised (official) representative

- `FACT` The University of Nottingham states it "works with official representatives around the world – such as education agencies and counsellors" and that "All recruitment agents used by The University of Nottingham are carefully selected and appointed, and all are listed on the University's website." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://nottingham.ac.uk/studywithus/international-applicants/contact-us/representatives/representatives.aspx
- `FACT` The University of Sheffield publishes country-level "Representatives in your region" pages. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.sheffield.ac.uk/international/countries/africa/east-africa/uganda/reps (example page; check for a Nigeria equivalent)
- `FACT` Goldsmiths publishes an "International Agent Policy" and an "Information for prospective international representatives and agents" page. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.gold.ac.uk/international/representatives/prospective/ ; https://www.gold.ac.uk/media/docs/prospective-students/global-opportunities/International-Agent-Policy-(PDF).pdf
- `INFERENCE` In UK university usage "official/authorised representative" **is** the appointed, contracted agent; universities publish lists of them so that students can check. A claim to be a university's representative is therefore externally verifiable (and falsifiable) by any student who checks the university's list. Making that claim without appearing on the list is the single highest-risk statement the site could make (see §4.1 on the DMCC Act 2024 banned practice).

### 1.5 Counsellor (school / independent)

- `FACT` The British Council runs a combined "UK agent **and counsellor** training" programme and maintains a "Database of Certified Agents and Counsellors". `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.britishcouncil.org/education/education-agents/training-agents
- `INFERENCE` The sector distinguishes a *counsellor* (advises students, typically in a school or independently, not contracted to providers) from an *agent* (contracted to providers). An independent application-support service that charges students and has no provider contracts sits closest to the "independent counsellor" category. This is the most defensible self-description available to the business today.

### 1.6 UCAS "registered centre" / adviser

- `FACT` UCAS states that "Schools, educational advisers and agents who assist students with UCAS Undergraduate applications to UK higher education can register themselves with UCAS." Registration "is completely free", gives tools to manage applications and lets providers "track where students have come from." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.ucas.com/advisers/help-and-training/guides-resources-and-training/international-advisers ; https://www.ucas.com/corporate/agents-ukeuinternational
- `FACT` For EU/international advisers UCAS requires "a reference or letter of recommendation from a UK university or college which is a member of UCAS **who you have been working with for at least 12 months**", stating contact details for both parties and how long the university has known the organisation. Queries: adviserhelp@ucas.ac.uk. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.ucas.com/advisers/help-and-training/guides-resources-and-training/international-advisers ; https://www.ucas.com/sites/default/files/international-undergraduate-guide-advisers.pdf
- `INFERENCE` A UCAS registered-centre number is an **operational** status (it lets the centre submit/track applications and attach references). It is not a quality mark, not an endorsement by UCAS, and not a university appointment. A centre must not describe itself as "UCAS-approved" or "UCAS-accredited".
- `INFERENCE` The 12-month working-relationship reference requirement means the business is **not yet eligible** to register as an international centre on the strength of an informal university contact. Students can instead apply as individuals ("apply independently") on UCAS Hub; the business can support that without being a centre. `NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION` for the independent-applicant mechanics — confirm on ucas.com.

### 1.7 Sub-agents, aggregators, brokers, marketplaces

- `FACT` The AQF "recommends that providers work with their contracted agents to ensure transparency around the use of sub-agents", and an agent's endorsement of the National Code and training "also applies to any sub-agent partners working with the contracted agent." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.britishcouncil.org/education/agents-counsellors/uk-quality-agent-framework
- `FACT` Coventry University expects agents working with sub-agents "to be transparent around the use of sub-agents and train sub-agents in accordance with the National Code". `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.coventry.ac.uk/partner-hub/agent-requirements/
- `INFERENCE` Becoming a **sub-agent** of an already-appointed Nigerian agency is a realistic near-term route to legitimate "we work with an appointed representative of X" wording — but only with a written sub-agent agreement, and the wording must say *sub-agent of [Agency]*, not *representative of [University]*.

## 2. UK frameworks governing agents

### 2.1 Agent Quality Framework (AQF) — *sector-led, now referenced by Home Office guidance*

- `FACT` The UK AQF "was established in 2022 following extensive research with students, education providers, education advisers and agents" and is "jointly led by the British Council, BUILA, UKCISA and Universities UK International (UUKi)." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.buila.ac.uk/aqf ; https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/our-work/working-with-agents/ ; https://www.britishcouncil.org/education/agents-counsellors/uk-quality-agent-framework
- `FACT` Four principles: "empowering student choice and enabling informed decision making; ensuring good agent governance and professionalism; promoting ethical agent practices, professional knowledge and competency; and increasing transparency and accountability." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://www.buila.ac.uk/aqf
- `FACT` Tools: UK agent and counsellor engagement hub; UK agent and counsellor training; Database of Certified Agents and Counsellors; National Code of Ethical Practice; Good Practice Guide for Providers Using Education Agents; Good Practice Guide for UK Education Agents Partnering for Quality; Student Guide to Choosing an Education Agent. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://www.buila.ac.uk/aqf ; https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/news/agent-quality-framework-resources/
- `FACT` "Over 150 institutions have signed up to the AQF pledge" (BUILA; figure date unknown). `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://www.buila.ac.uk/aqf
- `FACT` 2026 commentary exists but was **not read**: HEPI, "The Agent Quality Framework and the optics of compliance" (17 May 2026) https://www.hepi.ac.uk/2026/05/17/weekend-reading-the-agent-quality-framework-and-the-optics-of-compliance/ ; The PIE News, "UK Agent Quality Framework to add extra risk framework" https://thepienews.com/uk-agent-quality-framework-to-add-extra-risk-framework/ . `NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION` — read both before relying on any statement about a 2026 "risk framework".

### 2.2 National Code of Ethical Practice for UK Education Agents — *sector code, now evidence-required by Home Office guidance*

- `FACT` Underlying ethical requirements: integrity; objectivity; professional competence and due care; **transparency — "declaring conflicts of interest to all clients, especially when service fees are charged to both the education provider and the prospective student"**; confidentiality; professional behaviour; professionalism and purpose. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.buila.ac.uk/uploads/docs/National-Code-of-Ethical-Practice-for-UK-Education-Agents-FINAL-Flag-version-2.pdf
- `FACT` Universities operationalise it: Coventry expects agents to "adhere to and host the National Code of Ethical Practice on their website", "display the British Council's Student Guide to Choosing an Education Agent", "proactively send a copy of the Student Guide to all students", and have "a minimum of one counsellor per branch" holding valid British Council certification. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://www.coventry.ac.uk/partner-hub/agent-requirements/
- `FACT` University of Westminster publishes an "Agent Policy and Code of Conduct 2024". `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://www.westminster.ac.uk/sites/default/public-files/general-documents/Agent-Policy-and-Code-of-Conduct-2024.pdf
- `RECOMMENDATION` Even as a non-agent, voluntarily adopt the Code's transparency principle now: publish fees, state that no commission is received from any university (while true), and link to the British Council *Student Guide to Choosing an Education Agent*. This is cheap, honest, and positions the business for later appointment.

### 2.3 British Council UK agent and counsellor training and certificate — *voluntary; universities increasingly require it of agents*

- `FACT` The training "is 100% online and it is free of charge"; modules "can be taken in any order, at any time"; on completion and confirming understanding of the National Code "you will receive an online certificate and digital badge from the British Council", "valid for two years"; certified people "need to recertify every two years" and are "included in the Database of Certified Agents and Counsellors". `VERIFY-ON-PAGE`
  `SOURCE` https://www.britishcouncil.org/education/education-agents/training-agents ; https://www.britishcouncil.org.gh/Become-a-UK-agent-or-counsellor (Ghana page; a Nigeria page at britishcouncil.org.ng is expected but was `NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION`)
- `RECOMMENDATION` The founder(s) should complete this certificate **now**. It is free, it is the item universities (e.g. BCU, Coventry) ask for as "proof of education agent training", and it is a *truthful* credential the site can display — phrased as "British Council certified UK agent & counsellor training (individual certificate, valid to [date])", never as "British Council approved agency".

- `FACT` The white paper *Restoring control over the immigration system* (May 2025) proposed: mandatory AQF participation "for any sponsor using recruitment agents for overseas students"; raising each Basic Compliance Assessment (BCA) threshold by five percentage points (course enrolment ≥95%, course completion ≥90%); and a Red-Amber-Green banding of sponsor BCA performance. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` — the snippets are from law-firm summaries; confirm against the GOV.UK document.
  `SOURCE` (official, `NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION` URL) https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/restoring-control-over-the-immigration-system ; (secondary summaries retrieved) https://newlandchase.com/immigration-white-paper-key-updates-for-student-sponsors/ ; https://www.bevanbrittan.com/insights/articles/2025/higher-education-today-may-2025/
- `FACT` Home Office *Student sponsor guidance – Document 2: Sponsorship duties* (Jan 2026 v0.1) is published on GOV.UK. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/695fa33b027e0aa1765a5abc/Student_Sponsor_Guidance_-_Doc_2_-_Sponsorship_Duties_-_Jan_2026_-_v0.1.pdf
- `FACT` Updated sponsor guidance published **7 April 2026** requires that "all student sponsors using recruitment agents must have committed to adhering to the key principles of the Agent Quality Framework (AQF)" and "must retain evidence of how they are managing agents in line with the AQF and The National Code of Ethical Practice for UK Education Agents". `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` (secondary summaries; confirm in the GOV.UK guidance text)
  `SOURCE` https://monitor.icef.com/2026/04/uk-home-office-publishes-updated-visa-sponsor-guidance-for-agents-and-third-parties/ ; https://doyleclayton.co.uk/recruitment-agents-new-responsibilities-for-student-sponsors/ ; https://axis.lawyer/student-sponsor-guidance-2026/
- `INFERENCE` **Consequence for the business:** since April 2026 a university that appoints an agent must be able to evidence its oversight of that agent. Universities will therefore be *more* selective and demand *more* documentation (training certificate, business registration, references, Code adherence) before appointing anyone. An informal contact will not convert into an appointment without the business meeting these evidential requirements.

## 4. Legal / consumer points for a UK- or Nigerian-facing paid service

> All interpretation below is `INFERENCE`. `RECOMMENDATION`: obtain written advice from (a) a UK solicitor on consumer law and data protection and (b) a Nigerian legal practitioner on FCCPA and NDPA before taking payment from students.

### 4.1 UK: false claims of approval / endorsement — Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024

- `FACT` The DMCC Act 2024 came into force for consumer-protection purposes on 6 April 2025 and lists 32 commercial practices that are in all circumstances unfair (Schedule 20). `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20 ; https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/digital-markets-competition-and-consumers-act-2024-in-force-now-3/
- `FACT` Schedule 20 para 4 bans "claiming that a trader, a trader's commercial practice, or a product has been approved, endorsed or authorised by a public or private body when the claim is false, or the terms of the approval, endorsement or authorisation have not been, or are not being, complied with"; para 3 bans displaying a trust mark or quality mark without authorisation. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20
- `INFERENCE` Describing the business as a university's "representative/partner/agent", or as "UCAS-approved"/"British Council-approved", without the corresponding appointment would fall squarely within these banned practices if the business trades in/with the UK. This is the legal basis for the phrasing rules in §5.

### 4.2 UK: Consumer Rights Act 2015 (services)

- `FACT` Every contract to supply a service includes a term that the trader must perform the service with reasonable care and skill (s.49); information given by the trader about the service or trader that the consumer takes into account is binding (s.50); terms excluding liability under ss.49–50 are not binding (s.57). `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/part/1/chapter/4 ; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/57/enacted ; https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/consumer-rights-act-2015/consumer-rights-act-2015
- `INFERENCE` Because pre-contract statements become binding terms, every claim on the site about what the service delivers (e.g. "we review your personal statement within 5 days") is enforceable. Avoid outcome promises ("we get you an offer") entirely. Whether the CRA applies to a Nigerian consumer contracting with a Nigerian entity is a conflict-of-laws question — professional advice required.
- `NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION` Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 (14-day cancellation for distance contracts) — expected to apply to online sales to UK consumers; check https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134.

### 4.3 Nigeria: Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Act 2018 (FCCPA) / FCCPC

- `FACT` The FCCPA 2018 establishes the Federal Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (FCCPC) and the Competition and Consumer Protection Tribunal; it prohibits "misleading, deceptive, or unfair business practices" and "empowers consumers to seek redress and compels service providers to meet lawful standards of fairness and service delivery." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://fccpc.gov.ng/lagos-enugu-judgments-reinforce-consumer-protection-under-fccpa-tunji-bello/ ; https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/consumer-law/1504638/consumer-protection-regulation-in-nigeria-the-role-of-the-federal-competition-and-consumer-protection-commission
- `DATA UNAVAILABLE` Specific section numbers on false/misleading representations and the consumer's right to information were not retrieved (search budget). `NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION` — check the Act text at https://fccpc.gov.ng (Act PDF) before citing sections.
- `INFERENCE` A Nigerian-registered service selling to Nigerian consumers is squarely within FCCPC jurisdiction; misrepresenting university affiliation would be a misleading practice under the FCCPA just as under the UK DMCC Act. The practical rule is the same in both jurisdictions.

### 4.4 UK GDPR / ICO registration

- `FACT` "If your business processes personal data as a controller, you must register with the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) and pay a data protection fee unless an exemption applies." Fees from 17 February 2025: £52 (tier 1 / small), £78 (tier 2), £3,763 (tier 3). Keeping customer contact details or running a mailing list can trigger the obligation; processors (acting only on a controller's instructions) do not pay. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/getting-started-with-gdpr/data-protection-fee-what-you-need-to-do/ ; https://www.gov.uk/data-protection-register-notify-ico-personal-data ; https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/data-protection-fee-regime-proposed-changes/outcome/data-protection-fee-regime-government-response
- `INFERENCE` If the business is (or becomes) a UK-established entity, or offers services to people in the UK / monitors them (UK GDPR Art. 3 — `NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION`), it is a controller of student data (academic records, passport details, finances — some of it special-category health data for medicine applicants) and must pay the ICO fee and meet UK GDPR duties (privacy notice, lawful basis, international transfer safeguards for Nigeria→UK/UK→Nigeria flows).

### 4.5 Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 (NDPA) / NDPC

- `FACT` NDPA s.2 applies where the controller/processor is "domiciled in, resident in, or operating in Nigeria", where processing "occurs within Nigeria", **or** where the controller/processor "is not domiciled in, resident in, or operating in Nigeria but is processing personal data of a data subject in Nigeria." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf (NDPC General Application and Implementation Directive 2025) ; https://fpf.org/blog/nigerias-new-data-protection-act-explained/
- `FACT` The NDPC GAID 2025 clarifies that s.2(c) covers a foreign controller that "processes the personal data of a data subject in Nigeria **or targets** the personal data of data subjects in Nigeria." `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` `SOURCE` https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/NDP-ACT-GAID-2025-MARCH-20TH.pdf ; https://templars-law.com/app/uploads/2025/04/NDPC-Issues-the-Nigeria-Data-Protection-Act-General-Application-and-Implementation-Directive-2025.pdf
- `FACT` NDPA s.44 requires "data controllers and data processors of major importance" to register with the NDPC; s.65 defines them by reference to thresholds the Commission prescribes; the NDPC Guidance Notice sets a threshold of personal data of **more than 200 data subjects within six months** (or specified sectors), with three tiers and registration fees reported in the range NGN 10,000–250,000. `VERIFY-ON-PAGE` (threshold and fees from secondary summaries) `SOURCE` https://www.mondaq.com/nigeria/data-protection/1434270/key-highlights-of-the-guidance-notice-on-registration-of-data-controllers-and-data-processors-of-major-importance ; https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ng/pdf/2024/03/Nigeria%20Data%20Protection%20Commission%E2%80%99s%20Guidance%20Notice%20on%20Registration%20of%20Data%20ProcessorsControllers%20of%20Major%20Importance.pdf
- `INFERENCE` The NDPA applies to this business **whichever country it is incorporated in**, because its entire audience is data subjects in Nigeria. Once it handles data of more than ~200 students in six months (a modest enquiry form would do this), it is likely a "controller of major importance" and must register with the NDPC and comply with the GAID (privacy policy, DPO/contact, annual audit filings — `NOT VERIFIED THIS SESSION`).

### 4.6 Compliance checklist (RECOMMENDATION — before taking the first payment)

1. Decide and document the legal entity(ies): Nigeria (CAC) and/or UK (Companies House). This drives everything below.
2. ICO fee registration if UK-established or UK-targeting; NDPC registration when the 200-subject threshold is approached (treat as inevitable).

### 5.3 Documentation ladder — what unlocks which claim

| Claim the site wants to make | Minimum documentation required (all must be on file and current) |
|---|---|
| "Sub-agent of [Nigerian agency], an appointed representative of [University]" | Signed sub-agent agreement with the master agent **plus** written confirmation (email suffices only if from the university) that the master agent is currently appointed and permits sub-agents; BC certificate; adherence to National Code |
| "Referral partner / introducer for [University]" | Signed referral/introducer agreement from the university (not an individual's email), stating scope and term |
| "Appointed agent / official representative of [University]" | Signed agent agreement; business appears on the university's published representatives list (check and screenshot); BC certificate (≥1 per branch); National Code hosted on site; Student Guide displayed and sent to every student; sub-agent policy; business registration (CAC/Companies House); references |
| "UCAS registered centre" | UCAS centre registration confirmation and centre number; for international centres, the 12-month reference letter from a UCAS-member university |
| "British Council certified" | Individual certificate(s) in date; phrase as individual training, never as agency approval |
| Displaying a university logo | Written brand-use permission from the university's marketing team |
