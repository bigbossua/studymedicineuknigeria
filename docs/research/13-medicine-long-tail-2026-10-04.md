## Official statements seen (VERIFY-ON-PAGE)
- **Medical Training (Prioritisation) Act 2026 (2026 c. 7)**: "UK medical graduate" means a holder of a primary UK qualification under the Medical Act 1983, excluding anyone who spent all or most of the training for it outside the British Islands. https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/7. Recorded as `gmc-registration.prioritisation_act`; the old proposal fact is archived. Royal Assent date and application to Foundation allocation are from BMA pages (not FACT).
- **GOV.UK Student visa money**: a snippet showed £1,529 a month in London and £1,171 outside London; we hold £1,483 and £1,136, now flagged `SOURCE_CHANGED`. The "doctor or dentist in training" exemption wording must be read in full before any page mentions it.
- **Birmingham** Nigeria Outstanding Achievement and Africa High Fliers scholarships: MBChB and BDS applicants are not eligible.
- **Manchester** Nigeria page: WASSCE holders need a recognised foundation programme; A-levels or IB for direct entry; NCUK International Foundation Year in Abuja, Lagos and Port Harcourt; WAEC English at B3 or B2 depending on the course, within seven years.
- **QMUL Malta**: MBBS core curriculum identical to London, degree awarded by Queen Mary; read with the Act, mostly-Malta training falls outside the UK-graduate definition.
- **Buckingham**: medical school intakes normally January and September.
- Fee-status pages (UWE, Exeter): home fees generally need settled status plus three years' ordinary residence.

## Misinformation patterns to correct (never repeat them)
- "Free" or fully funded UK medicine for Nigerians; Commonwealth scholarships described as covering undergraduates.
- OND/HND "may allow direct entry" presented in a medicine context.
- Nigerian O'Level credit logic (C6 is a credit) applied to UK medicine entry, where WAEC is the GCSE layer and English evidence only.
- Lists of schools that "accept WASSCE" without the A-level or foundation condition.
- BMAT and "SAT subject tests" named as current tests.
- Stale fees (2014–15 and 2017–2020 figures) returned for 2026 questions; wrong course lengths ("four years").
- "GMC approval" of a Nigerian university's MBBS presented as UK recognition.
- A planned Lagos campus of a UK university presented as offering UK medicine degrees, without a UK university source.
- Offshore UK-awarded degrees sold as equivalent for UK training priority.
- The visa funds exemption for "doctors or dentists in training" quoted as if it covered undergraduate medical students.
