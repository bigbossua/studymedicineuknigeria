## Summary

1. **Most UK universities that recruit internationally run contracted agent networks and pay those agents commission on enrolment, but almost none say anything public about Medicine specifically.** For 25 of the 28 institutions in scope (UCLan and "Central Lancashire" are the same university), no Medicine-specific agent or commission statement was found. The exceptions are Edinburgh, Brunel and an unconfirmed Birmingham lead (points 2–4).
2. **One explicit Medicine exclusion was found (lead):** the University of Edinburgh's agent guidance lists "MBChB (Undergraduate Medicine)" among programmes that are **ineligible for commission**. Two separate searches returned the same wording; the page could not be opened.
3. **One possible exclusion is unconfirmed:** one search summary said the University of Birmingham excludes "Medicine and Surgery MBChB" from the programmes its agents can recruit to; a follow-up extended search did not corroborate it and the source page was not identified. Treat as unverified.
4. **One positive Medicine signal (lead):** Brunel University of London publishes an "Agent slides Medicine 2024" PDF on its medical school site, and a search summary of its agent page says it has approved agents who deal with Medicine applications.
5. **Glasgow (outside the list, included for contrast; lead):** its Medicine admissions page reportedly says the university is not permitted to liaise with anyone other than the applicant about application-specific matters.
6. **Several schools in scope take no international Medicine students at undergraduate entry** (Anglia Ruskin, Edge Hill, Lincoln, and per one lead Sunderland; see doc 06), so the agent question does not arise for them.
7. **Becoming an agent** follows a common pattern on published university pages (lead): application form, two references (often from other institutions already contracting the agent), proof of business registration, proof of agent training (British Council certificate, sometimes ICEF), commitment to the National Code, then a signed agreement with targets and listing on the university's representatives page. Some universities state they are not appointing new agents, and one (Goldsmiths; no Medicine) reportedly is not processing agent submissions from Nigeria.
8. **Conflicts of interest:** the National Code requires agents to declare conflicts of interest to all clients, "especially when service fees are charged to both the education provider and the prospective student", to be transparent about which services are "educational institution pays" and which "student pays", and to hold signed written agreements with student clients that include fees (lead). Our service charges students, so taking commission would trigger every one of these duties and would make the current "we receive none" statement on *Our status* untrue.

### 2.2 Common pattern (`INFERENCE` from the pages above and doc 11 §3)

1. A registered legal entity (Nigeria: CAC certificate; UK: Companies House) and professional premises.
2. References, usually two, often from UK institutions that already contract the agent. **A first-time agency with no existing contracts struggles to meet this.**
3. Individual British Council UK agent and counsellor certificate(s) (sometimes ICEF training accepted).
4. Signed commitment to the National Code and the university's own agent code of conduct.
5. Due-diligence review, then a signed agreement with targets, commission schedule and programme exclusions.
6. Listing on the university's country "representatives" page. This listing is how students check a claim.
7. Ongoing monitoring under the AQF: conversion, visa refusal and withdrawal rates, complaints, training renewal.

### 2.3 Sub-agent route

- `lead-not-read` The AQF expects contracted agents to be transparent about sub-agents, and the agent's training and National Code commitment "also applies to any sub-agent partners" (doc 11 §1.7, British Council AQF page).
- `lead-not-read` St Andrews does not work with any agent, counsellor or sub-agent whose details are not on its official pages.
- `INFERENCE` Working as a sub-agent of an appointed Nigerian agency is possible only where the university allows sub-agents and the master agent discloses them; it would make our service a sub-agent, which *Our status* currently says we are not.

### 2.4 UCAS registered centre (separate from university appointment)

- `lead-not-read` UCAS has a separate registration form for **commercial organisations that charge for their service** (to applicants or universities). Before completing an application on someone's behalf, an agent must have "all necessary authority" and give the applicant enough time to read the applicant declaration, terms and privacy policy. https://www.ucas.com/media/225421/download
- From doc 11: international centres need a reference from a UCAS-member university they have worked with for at least 12 months.

## 3. Sector frameworks and UK rules (Question 3)

### 3.1 National Code of Ethical Practice for UK Education Agents (sector code)

- `lead-not-read` Applies to "education agents and agent aggregators, brokers and marketplaces representing UK education providers" (British Council wording adds "on the register of licensed student sponsors"). 2021 version: https://www.buila.ac.uk/uploads/docs/National-Code-of-Ethical-Practice-for-UK-Education-Agents-FINAL-Flag-version-2.pdf ; mirror: https://universitiesuk.ac.uk/sites/default/files/uploads/UUKi/Documents%20-%20misc/National%20Code%20of%20Ethical%20Practice%20for%20UK%20Education%20Agents.pdf
- `lead-not-read` Ethical framework: integrity; objectivity (judgement not compromised by bias or conflict of interest); professional competence and due care; **transparency: "declaring conflicts of interest to all clients, especially when service fees are charged to both the education provider and the prospective student"**; confidentiality; professional behaviour.
- `secondary` (the only copy found is on a third-party agent's website, not an official host) A **July 2025 version** has five standards: Organisational behaviours; Student-centred practice; Organisational competence; Objective advice and guidance; Ethical business practice. Search summary: agents must be transparent about which services are "educational institution pays" (commission) and which "student pays", and must hold **signed written or digital agreements with student clients** covering fees, information about the agent and the complaints process. https://www.ccoex.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ThenationalcodeforUKeducationagentsJuly2025.pdf. **The official location of the July 2025 version was not found.** Find it on BUILA or the British Council before citing it.

### 3.2 UK Agent Quality Framework (AQF)

- `lead-not-read` Established 2022; jointly led by the British Council, BUILA, UKCISA and Universities UK International; four principles: empowering student choice and informed decision-making; good agent governance and professionalism; ethical agent practices, knowledge and competency; transparency and accountability. Tools include the National Code, the agent and counsellor training, the Database of Certified Agents and Counsellors, good-practice guides for providers and for agents, and the Student Guide to Choosing an Education Agent. https://www.britishcouncil.org/education/agents-counsellors/uk-quality-agent-framework ; https://www.buila.ac.uk/aqf
- `lead-not-read` The AQF is a commitment made by **providers** (universities sign a pledge). An agent does not sign the AQF; it works to the National Code under a provider's AQF-aligned management.
- `secondary` Updated Home Office student sponsor guidance, published 7 April 2026, reportedly requires every student sponsor using recruitment agents to have committed to the AQF's key principles and to keep evidence of managing agents in line with the AQF and the National Code. Secondary sources: https://monitor.icef.com/2026/04/uk-home-office-publishes-updated-visa-sponsor-guidance-for-agents-and-third-parties/ ; https://doyleclayton.co.uk/recruitment-agents-new-responsibilities-for-student-sponsors/ . GOV.UK could not be reached; the January 2026 Document 2 URL is in doc 11 §2.4. **Confirm on GOV.UK before repeating.**
- `INFERENCE` Since that guidance, universities carry compliance risk for their agents' conduct, so they will look harder at any new agent, especially one that also charges students.

### 3.3 British Council UK agent and counsellor training

- `lead-not-read` Free and online; on completion, and after confirming understanding of the National Code, the person receives an online certificate and digital badge **valid for two years**, then must recertify; certified people may appear on the public Database of Certified Agents and Counsellors. The search summary says the certificate and badge **apply to the individual, not to the organisation they work for**. https://www.britishcouncil.org/education/education-agents/training-agents ; Nigeria page: https://www.britishcouncil.org.ng/study-uk/uk-education-agents-online-course
- `INFERENCE` It is a training credential. It does not approve an agency and does not create any relationship with a university (as already stated on *Our status*).

### 3.4 Other UK and Nigerian rules

- `lead-not-read` A UK university AQF page (search result set including Wrexham and Westminster) says the UK does not require a licence for agents recruiting to UK providers, though local licences may apply in the agent's country. https://wrexham.ac.uk/international-students/agent-quality-framework/
- **Consumer law on dual charging:** no UK public body was found that sets a specific disclosure rule for agents charging both student and university (`not found`). The relevant general rules are in doc 11 §4: DMCC Act 2024 Schedule 20 bans false claims of approval or endorsement, and the Consumer Rights Act 2015 makes information the trader gives about itself binding. Both are `lead-not-read` and need a solicitor's confirmation.
- **Nigeria:** no Nigerian licensing regime for study-abroad agents was found (`not found`). FCCPA 2018 and NDPA 2023 apply generally (doc 11 §4.3, §4.5).

**No source found prohibits charging students while also receiving commission.** The duty found is to **disclose**. Individual agent agreements may restrict or ban student fees, so check each one before signing (`not found` publicly).

`RECOMMENDATION` (if the owner ever takes a commission-paying agreement):
1. Before any student pays, give a written, signed service agreement that states (a) our fee and what it covers, (b) **each university that pays us commission**, (c) that a university that pays us nothing (for example, Edinburgh for MBChB, if confirmed) gets the same advice, and (d) the complaints route. This fits the current owner decision that fees are not public: disclosure is to the client in the agreement, not on public pages.
2. Show the same disclosure on *Our status* (university name, agreement type, dates), as that page already promises.
3. Decide, and write down, whether commission is offset against the student's fee. No source requires this; it is the owner's choice.
4. Keep the eligibility check and school recommendations based on published entry requirements only, and record in each case file why each school was suggested, so objectivity can be shown.

## 5. What we must not claim

Until a **signed agreement** exists **and** is listed on *Our status* (name, agreement type, dates), the site, social posts, ads, emails and staff must not use:

- "official representative", "authorised representative", "representative of [University]"
- "partner", "partnered with", "in partnership with", "recruitment partner", "university partner"
- "approved agent", "accredited agent", "agent of [University]", "appointed by"
- "referral partner", "introducer" (these need a signed referral agreement)
- "sub-agent of [agency]" (needs a signed sub-agent agreement and the university's knowledge)
- "British Council approved/accredited/certified agency" (the certificate belongs to an individual; at most say "[Name] holds the British Council UK agent and counsellor training certificate, valid to [date]")
- "UCAS approved", "UCAS registered" (we are not a registered centre)
- any university logo, crest or name used in a way that suggests a connection
- "we can get you a place at [University]", "priority" or "fast-track" processing, or any suggestion that using us improves offer chances (Sheffield and Newcastle both say using an agent does not affect the decision, lead)
- any commission rate, or any statement that a university does or does not pay commission for Medicine, unless the page has been read and the fact sits in `reference_facts` as verified.
